Sleep is one of those things that most of us only notice when it goes wrong - and when it does, the effect on every other area of life is immediate and cumulative. Concentration falters. Mood deteriorates. Physical health is affected. Decisions get worse.

And yet in clinical practice, insomnia is often handled with a prescription for zopiclone and a follow-up in two weeks - an approach that addresses the symptom while creating a new problem.

The evidence is clear about what actually works for chronic insomnia. It is not what most people expect, and it is not what most people receive.

## What counts as insomnia

Insomnia is defined as difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early - at least three nights per week, for at least three months - causing daytime impairment. Occasional bad nights are not insomnia. Chronic insomnia is a distinct clinical condition.

## Why we sleep badly - the main categories

Primary insomnia (psychophysiological insomnia): The most common type. Often starts with a stressful period but persists long after the trigger has resolved. The bed itself becomes associated with wakefulness and anxiety. A vicious cycle: worry about sleep leads to hyperarousal leads to poor sleep leads to more worry.

Mental health related: Anxiety causes difficulty falling asleep. Depression often causes early morning waking. Treating the underlying condition improves sleep.

Medical causes: Obstructive sleep apnoea, restless legs syndrome, chronic pain, nocturia, hyperthyroidism, and sleep disruption from medications.

Shift work: The circadian rhythm is powerful and deeply disrupted by irregular shift patterns. Particularly relevant for healthcare workers and security staff - groups that include a significant proportion of African diaspora workers in the UK.

## Case study: Josephine's three-year insomnia

Josephine, 47, a community nurse from Zimbabwe based in Bristol, came to see me after three years of poor sleep. She was on her second course of zopiclone. Her pattern was lying awake for 1-2 hours after going to bed, then waking at 3am unable to return to sleep.

Her sleep started deteriorating when her mother died in 2021. But the grief had passed. The sleep had never recovered.

This is classic psychophysiological insomnia. The trigger had gone, but the pattern had been learned. Her brain had associated bed with wakefulness.

I referred her for CBT-I. Eight sessions. Twelve weeks later she reported sleeping within 20 minutes of going to bed.

"They told me to sleep less at first," she said. "I could not believe that was the advice. But it worked."

## CBT-I - the treatment that actually fixes insomnia

CBT-I is the first-line recommended treatment for chronic insomnia according to NICE and virtually every major sleep medicine body worldwide. It outperforms sleeping pills in head-to-head studies, and its effects are durable.

Sleep restriction therapy: Counterintuitive but effective. A prescribed sleep window - initially shorter than you would like - builds sleep pressure and consolidates fragmented sleep.

Stimulus control: Retraining the brain to associate bed with sleep rather than wakefulness. Get out of bed if awake for more than 20 minutes. Maintain a consistent wake time regardless of how badly you slept.

Cognitive restructuring: Challenging catastrophic thoughts about sleep - I need 8 hours or I cannot function, I will never sleep properly again.

How to access CBT-I in the UK: NHS Talking Therapies; Sleepio - a digital CBT-I programme, NHS-funded in some areas; private therapists trained in CBT-I.

## The sleeping pill problem

Z-drugs (zopiclone, zolpidem): Effective for helping people fall asleep but tolerance develops within weeks. Dependency is common. Not recommended for longer than 2-4 weeks.

Alcohol: Helps most people fall asleep faster - then disrupts sleep architecture in the second half of the night, reducing REM sleep and causing more frequent waking. Regular alcohol use to aid sleep is a reliable route to worse sleep quality and dependency.
